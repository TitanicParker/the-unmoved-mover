# The Cause of the Effects — Primary Evidence Bundle

**Documentary compilation date:** 17 October 2026. **Subject:** clinical and institutional documentation concerning neurological care at Galway University Hospital, 2017–2025. **Method:** independent selection and transcription of accessible published repository source-record representations. The **underlying signed originals, scans, and native institutional audit logs have not been inspected within this compilation** unless clearly identified otherwise. Clinical extracts remain records of what was documented; they do not settle the clinical correctness of the documented opinions. This bundle is independent of the accompanying HTML account.

## Source hierarchy and conventions

Levels: **1** original signed document inspected; **2** repository reproduction described as a faithful redacted representation of an original; **3** contemporaneous clinician-authored record reproduced/transcribed; **4** later clinician statement or institutional report of past events; **5** canonical repository transcription of source records, without native source verification in this compilation; **6** repository analysis. No analytical text is treated as source evidence. In this file, an authentic-looking reproduction is not asserted to be a verified native original. `[Redacted]`, OCR irregularities, and copied documentary headings belong to published sources where so indicated. **CLIN identifiers** are repository-created cross-reference labels, not assumed to be native hospital record identifiers.

**Privacy and fidelity:** Published source pages are redacted; this file carries their redactions, and deliberately omits unrelated direct identifiers in reproduced canonical sections. Where omitted text cannot be verified from a source, the fact is disclosed. The bracketed lines identifying source scope and limitations are compilation annotations, not clinical words.

```text
Genetics

I have explained to him the difficulties with the genetic assessment.

One of the chief difficulties with this is the question of whether a firm genetic diagnosis
will actually actively change management?

This question is (in the majority of cases) usually answered in the negative.

In a way to explain genetics, I have in a very simplistic manner broken it up Into 3
different concepts:

-#1 what I term for [Patient]‘s understanding as “strong genetics”

---where genetic disorders run very strongly through a family. We would terms these
usually Autosomal Dominant.This is clearly not the case in [Patient]*s case and therefore it
is not likely that these apply. It would not apply to most individuals with Parkinson's
either. It is not a worry as he has no children as yet.

#2 what I term for for [Patient]’s understanding as "moderate genetics"

---I use this example to explain where a person may have a range of different genes,
many of them are currently unknown but which confer “susceptibilities” to Parkinson's.
Depending on the combination of susceptibilities a person has genetically, then these,
and various components of environment can contribute to the development of a
Parkinson’ s disorder. This is a theory that probably applies to most people who actually
develop Parkinson's disease “sporadically” or “idiopathically” who may have
susceptibility genes that we do not understand the role of as yet or known genes with
variations (e.g. In the LRRK2/ SNCA / GBA genes), as well as a range of possible
environmental factors.

#3---what I term for [Patient]’s understandinf as "weak genetics"

---by this we mean the classical autosomal recessively inherited disorders that can
occur within a family but which would be very unlikely to affect other family members.
It is quite plausible that [Patient] may have one of these disorders as the cause for his
Parkinsons/Parkinsonisms.
```

# Formal Complaint – Diagnostic Anchoring, Bias, and Failure to Reassess (2017–2025)

To Whom It May Concern,

My name is Liam Moloney.

I am writing to raise serious concerns regarding the clinical handling of my case by Galway East Medical Practice (Dr. Aaron Leo Brennan) and the Neurology Department at Galway University Hospitals (Dr. Thomas Monaghan) during the period 2017 to 2025.

I submit this complaint with respect for the complexity of clinical decision-making, but also with a sincere request for accountability, transparency, and learning—both in relation to my case and potentially others affected by similar processes.

### Background

My presenting symptom—sharp, pressure-sensitive foot pain—began in 2005. By 2017, I sought medical intervention through Galway East Medical Practice.

From that point, the pain was consistently attributed to peripheral neuropathy, likely alcohol-related, and this framing appears to have followed me into Neurology referrals and specialist reviews.

For eight years, despite continued reporting of pain and its impact, the working diagnosis did not materially change, nor was it re-evaluated against emerging symptoms or alternative explanations, including Parkinson's-related pain syndromes or dopamine-responsive conditions.

### Concerns Raised

I make this complaint fully aware that I do not have access to every internal note, referral, or correspondence generated over these years. However, I am now left with deep concern that the following occurred, or that the system made it impossible to detect if they did:

1. **Diagnostic Anchoring and Bias**
   - Early framing of my condition as alcohol-related neuropathy became fixed, potentially precluding further investigation.

2. **Failure to Reassess**
   - Despite deterioration, ongoing pain, and functional impairment, no fresh neurological workup was undertaken that considered Parkinson’s-related pain syndromes or tested dopaminergic responsiveness.

3. **Potential Hierarchical Influence**
   - I have reason to believe senior clinical influence may have advised caution against certain treatments, which—combined with referral bias—closed off viable therapeutic trials.

### The Turning Point

In March 2025, I independently trialed Sinemet CR. The result was immediate and sustained relief from two decades of disabling foot pain—effectively disproving the prior working diagnosis and suggesting my condition was dopamine-responsive all along.

This raises a fundamental question: Why was this possibility not explored earlier, given the magnitude of harm endured?

### The Harm Caused

- Physical: Prolonged, preventable pain
- Psychological: Fear of movement, cumulative distress, and erosion of resilience
- Loss of Trust: A profound impact on my confidence in clinical systems' ability to self-correct

### Request for Review and Disclosure

I make no presumptions about individual intent or malice. My concern is systemic: that processes of diagnostic framing, confirmation bias, and deference to hierarchy may have overridden patient experience and clinical curiosity.

I respectfully request:

1. An independent review of my case, with attention to the clinical reasoning applied from 2017 to 2025.
2. Full disclosure of my medical records, referrals, specialist correspondence, and any internal memos related to my case during this period.
3. A written response explaining how this outcome was possible, and what measures exist to prevent similar diagnostic anchoring from persisting unchecked in other cases.
4. Preservation of all records related to my case, including internal communications, pending my full review.

### Closing Statement

I submit this complaint not in anger, but from a place of humility and genuine need for understanding.

I recognise the pressures and complexities of medical practice. However, after eight years of persistent pain and the eventual discovery that relief was possible, I am compelled to ask how the system could have missed this—despite my continued engagement.

My aim is not confrontation, but to ensure learning, accountability, and the protection of future patients from similar harm.

Yours sincerely,  
Liam Moloney
